# Build authority on LinkedIn with proprietary evidence

**Scope caveat: every finding below rests on web-search result summaries. Page fetching was blocked by the network allowlist, so no primary page (LinkedIn, Edelman, Buffer, Wikipedia, Socialinsider, Originality.ai, van der Blom) was opened. Figures are second-hand, many sources are vendors selling a product, and all numbers should be verified before they are hard-coded into a skill.** With that limit, the best-supported rule for a Google/Meta performance marketer building authority is to publish first-party evidence: real account experiments, teardowns, and measurement judgments with baselines, caveats, and honest nulls. Buyers say demonstrating understanding of their problems builds trust more than product strength (56% vs 14%), and 84% now use AI to summarize thought leadership, which makes generic advice a commodity and proprietary data the scarce asset ([Edelman 2025 via search summary](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report); [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/edelman-ai-thought-leadership-buyers/)). LinkedIn says it is down-ranking generic AI content and engagement bait while still welcoming AI-assisted posts that carry original ideas ([LinkedIn News](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91545007/linkedin-declares-war-on-ai-slop)). Numeric algorithm folklore (golden hour, 3 hashtags, comment multipliers, 60% link penalty) has no official support. No source studied hiring managers, so that audience rests on inference.

Evidence key used throughout: **A** = stated by LinkedIn (seen only via secondary summaries); **B** = large third-party dataset, usually vendor-run, methods unseen; **C** = practitioner opinion or small/single-account test; **I** = my inference from sources; **[Unverified]** = no traceable source.

## Original evidence is the scarce asset buyers reward

Edelman-LinkedIn's 2025 study (1,934 executives) found 79% more likely to champion a vendor in an RFP if it consistently publishes quality thought leadership, and 59% of final decision-makers said weak or absent thought leadership contributed to a vendor being dropped ([Edelman 2026 page via search summary](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2026-b2b-thought-leadership-report)). The 2026 edition (about 1,135 respondents) reports 82% say thought leadership gains value as AI spreads, and buyers prize original data and human judgment AI answers lack ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/82-of-b2b-buyers-value-thought-leadership-more-as-ai-spreads-edelman-finds/)). Critics note the study does not show causation and that research is only original if you do not know the answer at the start ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/edelman-ai-thought-leadership-buyers/); [Gaskell](https://adigaskell.substack.com/p/edelman-report-downplays-the-strategic)). Edelman covers buyers, not recruiters; evidence on how hiring managers weigh LinkedIn content versus portfolios was not found.

Practitioner sources converge on a defensible point of view: one narrow, non-obvious position backed by first-hand cases, and contrarian only if it "rings true" ([Animalz](https://www.animalz.co/blog/thought-leadership-content/); [The Speaker Lab](https://thespeakerlab.com/blog/position-yourself-as-a-thought-leader/)). Search Engine Land experiment write-ups supply the norms for credible performance posts: a hypothesis, one isolated variable, enough conversions per variant, 1-2 week learning periods, and published non-significant results ([SEL](https://searchengineland.com/ppc-experimentation-vs-ppc-testing-448771); [SEL null-result case](https://searchengineland.com/a-case-study-for-delivering-performance-in-a-mature-google-ads-account-315176)). The experiment template (hypothesis, setup, baseline, result with significance, what you would change) is my inference from those norms (I).

Client results carry legal risk that platform policy does not. NDAs often bar disclosing even the existence of an engagement, and written approval is the recommended standard for metrics ([Terms.law](https://terms.law/NDA/scenarios/professional-services/agency-services-nda.html); [Mario Peshev](https://mariopeshev.com/answers/case-studies-digital-agencies/)); agencies have been forced to remove unapproved case studies ([Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/name-shame-why-industry-names-favorite-brands-cant-talk/1968948)). The FTC rule on reviews and testimonials (effective 21 Oct 2024) requires disclosure of generally expected results for atypical outcomes; "results not typical" disclaimers do not suffice ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)). No official Google or Meta policy on publishing case studies was found.

## Formats and structures: documents lead, structure advice is opinion

Document (PDF carousel) and multi-image posts lead engagement rate in Socialinsider's company-page data: documents 7.00% and multi-image 6.45% for 2025 data, versus text about 4.50% ([Socialinsider 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/linkedin_benchmarks_2026.pdf); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-shows-document-posts-on-linkedin-see-more-engagement/816551/)). AuthoredUp (621,833 posts) also names document/carousel best, while polls and video conflict across datasets: Metricool reports poll reach up 206%, LigoSocial reports polls at -50% engagement versus text ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin); [Metricool via SMT](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/press-release/20250402-metricools-2025-linkedin-study-reveals-content-shifts-video-dominates-po); [LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/research/linkedin-post-formats)). Socialinsider is company-page data, so rates may not transfer to a personal profile (I).

For length, AuthoredUp's 372,126 personal-profile posts show 1,301-2,500 characters earning 27% higher engagement than posts under 400 ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit)); Magnettu says 800-1,500 ([Magnettu](https://www.magnettu.com/en/blog/what-works-on-linkedin-in-2026)). The post limit is 3,000 characters ([Eden](https://eden.so/blog/linkedin-character-limits/)). No head-to-head study of structures (case study, teardown, contrarian, framework) exists; the sources only recommend a specific-number or contrarian hook, evidence-backed body, and a discussion-oriented close ([GTM Stack](https://gtmstack-production.pages.dev/playbooks/linkedin-posting-playbook)). I found no verified named performance-marketing creators or their post data. Newsletter-versus-post reach data does not exist in these sources.

## AI-slop tells are structural, and the penalty is perceived inauthenticity

Wikipedia's advice page for editors says no single sign proves AI authorship and warns that surface fixes do not make text good ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), via summaries; [ScriptGrain](https://scriptgrain.com/blog/wikipedia-signs-of-ai-writing)). Structure is better supported than single words: the Kobak et al. study of 14M PubMed abstracts found excess use of words like "delve", "crucial", "potential", and "findings" in 2024, but word spikes are relative and model-era specific ([arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016v5); [Language Log](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=64877)). Perception research supports the authenticity risk: disclosed AI authorship lowered trust, competence, and likability in a 261-person study, and a 1,970-person survey found both human and LLM raters penalized disclosed AI use ([arXiv 2510.24011](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24011); [CHIWORK 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01418v1)). Engagement evidence is thin and conflicting: the "45% less engagement" figure for likely-AI posts is detector-based vendor data, while the same coverage reports AI-draft, human-edited posts at 6.85% versus 6.22% for purely human ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/)). Prevalence claims (Originality.ai about 54% of long posts likely AI; Pangram 41% fully AI) are detector-dependent ([Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/pt/blog/ai-content-published-linkedin); [Website Planet](https://www.websiteplanet.com/news/linkedin-long-posts-ai)).

LinkedIn's stated test is value, not provenance: AI-assisted content is welcome with original ideas or meaningful conversation ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91545007/linkedin-declares-war-on-ai-slop)). A "Seems like AI slop" report option appeared around 30 July 2026 ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/7/linkedin-adds-a-seems-like-ai-slop-button-to-report-low-quality-ai-generated-content/)). The 94% detection and 40% fewer views figures are second-hand [Unverified]. Credible-writer technique converges on supplying your own specifics, voice samples, and numbers (the model may invent a number you do not provide), and editing clusters of tells rather than banning single words ([Your AI Copyeditor](https://youraicopyeditor.substack.com/p/stop-your-writing-from-sounding-like); [PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/4-reasons-your-writing-accidentally-sounds-ai-generated-and-how-to-fix-it/); [Bea Krause Sorio](https://beakrausesorio.substack.com/p/how-to-write-well-with-ai-without)). No controlled evidence shows any technique raises LinkedIn performance.

## Cadence, hooks, CTAs, and commenting rest on vendor data

Buffer's observational study (2M+ posts, 94,000+ accounts) found 2-5 posts per week sustainable, 6-10 faster growth, and gains continuing at 11+, with an Ordinal analysis showing per-post reach falling gently as frequency rises while weekly totals still increase ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/); [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-posting-frequency)). Best times cluster on Tuesday-Thursday late morning to early afternoon but shift between Sprout editions, and Sprout's sample is mostly brands ([Sprout](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-linkedin/); [Sprout 2025](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/?p=172827)); treat as a hypothesis to test.

The "see more" fold is about 210 characters on desktop and 140-150 on mobile, observed by vendors rather than specified by LinkedIn ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit); [LinkedInPreview](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-hook-preview)). Hook data conflicts: AuthoredUp medians put story hooks at 2.60%, contrarian 2.31%, question 2.16% ([Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-hooks)); a ConnectSafely sample of 1,000+ posts puts curiosity-gap and contrarian highest and questions lowest ([DEV](https://dev.to/belal_zahran/i-analyzed-1000-viral-linkedin-posts-heres-the-formula-5a3o)); Ciela found 94% of viral posts had a hook with a specific number among four elements ([Ciela](https://www.ciela.ai/blogs/viral-linkedin-posts-ai-analysis)). A CTA-led first line is among the worst ([Supergrow](https://growthtools.supergrow.ai/linkedin-growth-playbook)). Engagement-rate definitions differ, so figures are not comparable across sources.

On CTAs, LinkedIn names "comment to agree" prompts as low-value content ([LinkedIn News](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)), and a 2023 change reduced visibility for posts explicitly asking for reactions and bait polls ([SEL](https://searchengineland.com/linkedin-feed-less-polls-engagement-bait-384986)). "Comment X" DM-gated funnels are called flagged by marketing blogs, not confirmed by LinkedIn ([LinkedInPreview](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-engagement-bait-2026)). For commenting, the author replying in the first hour correlates with 67% more engagement in an unreviewed 50,000-post analysis ([DEV](https://dev.to/olams/i-analyzed-50000-linkedin-posts-heres-what-actually-gets-engagement-56l9)); other vendor figures (comment in first 30 minutes gets about 4x impressions; replies lift a comment up to 2.4x) were not traced to primaries ([Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/long-vs-short-linkedin-comments/)). LinkedIn is also cracking down on AI-generated comments ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/linkedin-is-fighting-back-against-ai-slop-and-ai-comments)). Claims of 3x inbound from commenting are anecdotal ([StartupGTM](https://startupgtm.substack.com/p/comment-os)). Profile-optimization statistics (for example 21x views with a photo) have no original study [Unverified].

## Skill-ready rule set

The tables below are formatted for a skill-creator build. Source column links are those cited above.

### Rules (with evidence level)

| # | Rule | Level | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Base each post on a real account, test, or judgment the user supplies; never let the model supply numbers. | R2 | Experiment post: hypothesis, setup, metric and baseline, result with significance caveat, what you would change; publish nulls. | I from C | [SEL](https://searchengineland.com/ppc-experimentation-vs-ppc-testing-448771) |
| R3 | Frame around a specific buyer problem (e.g., lead quality vs volume), not generic tips. | B (survey, secondary) | [Edelman 2025](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report) |

| R5 | Client data: require written permission; otherwise anonymize, index metrics (percent change), or use own/test accounts or public assets. ### Open gaps to verify before encoding

Open the primary pages: LinkedIn News (ImprovingTheFeed), LinkedIn engineering post of 12 March 2026, Edelman 2025/2026 reports, Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing", Buffer frequency study, Socialinsider 2026, MagicPost link study, Originality.ai and Pangram studies. Unresolved: hiring-manager preferences for LinkedIn content; named performance-marketing creators and their verified patterns; structure-level quantitative comparisons; personal-profile (not company-page) format data; controlled CTA, comment-timing, and first-comment-link tests; official Google Ads and Meta policy on case-study publication; current van der Blom report (paid; format-level data unseen); whether any technique lowers reader suspicion of AI authorship.

## Conclusion

The sourced picture is narrower than LinkedIn advice usually claims. The only rules with platform-level or survey-level backing are to supply original, specific, human-judged content and to avoid pods, bait, and generic AI prose; format, length, cadence, and hook numbers are vendor correlations that a skill should present as defaults to test against the user's own analytics. The most useful design implication is that the skill's main job is intake rather than generation: it should collect the user's real data, baseline, caveats, and client-permission status first, then draft, since originality and permissioned evidence are what neither the AI-slop filter nor the audience can be satisfied without.
