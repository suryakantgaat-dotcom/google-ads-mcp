# Ideas from creators

An idea pool, not a rulebook. Everything here is creator-stated, vendor-stated or from search summaries, so it sits at evidence level C or below. Use it to generate options, then test them with `measurement-log.md`. Copy structures, never wording. Source notes: `research_notes/LinkedIn creator examples/`.

## 2. Writing methods

- Start from your own experience and point of view, then use AI only for hook variations and tidying (Oladipo, Singh). This matches intake-first drafting.
- Write 5 to 10 hook variants, draft rough, cut filler, swap vague wording for specifics, polish the hook and formatting last ([Search summary]).
- Keep drafting and proofreading as separate passes ([Search summary]).
- Build a voice guide from 5 to 10 of your own best posts, with explicit banned phrases ([Search summary]). See `linkedin-voice-capture`.
- Turn client and sales calls into posts, keeping the client's exact phrasing. One creator reports about 5 ideas per recorded call (Tim Keen, single anecdote, [Self-reported]). Anonymise first.

## 3. Workflow routines

- **Swipe file of structures**, not wording, searchable by format and hook type. Capture ideas separately from drafting.
- **Comment list**: 20 to 30 accounts chosen by who your buyers read, not by follower count. 10 to 25 minutes a day of substantive comments, early on a target's post. One source sells automated commenting, which conflicts with R11 (no automation).
- **Schedule a week ahead** (Dubey). Rebuild one idea per format instead of pasting it: frameworks as carousels, longer reasoning as a newsletter. Expand a post that does well into a newsletter edition.
- **Cadence**: Oladipo posts about 4 times a week. Singh and Bullock argue high volume is unsustainable. Choose what you can keep up, then test (R10).

## 4. Where creators disagree

| Topic | Positions |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Buffer data: more posts correlate with more impressions. Singh and Bullock: high frequency is unsustainable or unnecessary. |
| Length | Meredith Farley's top follower-gaining posts averaged about 749 characters. Vendor blogs say 1,250 to 3,000. The skill's default (R6) is 1,200 to 1,500, from AuthoredUp; test shorter. |
| Relatable personal posts | Oladipo values them. Dubey says they brought reach, not leads. |
| Question hooks | Commonly recommended. Ranked last in the AuthoredUp analysis Oladipo cites. |
| Carousel aspect ratio | Some guides say 4:5, others 1:1. File-size limits are also reported as 10 MB or 100 MB. Check LinkedIn's current help page. |
| Carousel strength | Buffer data show it as the top format. One agency newsletter says carousels are losing their edge. Metrics and samples differ. Oladipo's own recent top posts were videos. |
