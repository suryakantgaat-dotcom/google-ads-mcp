# Client results: consent and disclosure checklist

Run before drafting any post that uses client or employer results. This is a prompt to confirm consent and framing. It is not legal advice. The legal sources below were outside the verification pass, and no official Google Ads or Meta policy on publishing case studies was found. Check the user's contract.

Why this matters: platform policy is not the main risk. The risk is contractual and regulatory. NDAs often bar disclosing even that an engagement exists, and a logo or name permission does not cover publishing metrics. Agencies have been made to remove unapproved case studies (Campaign).

## Ask the user, in order

1. Is the work for a client, an employer, or the user's own or test account? Own or test accounts need no client consent. Go to item 8.
2. Is there an NDA or confidentiality clause? Does it cover the client's existence, name or metrics? If unsure, treat as covered.
3. Is there written consent to publish **these specific metrics**? A verbal yes or a logo-use permission does not count. Written approval is the recommended standard (Peshev; Terms.law).
4. Did the client approve the final wording, not only the idea?
5. Does the post name the client, or could a reader identify them (industry plus city plus product plus numbers)?

## If consent is missing, offer these options

- **Anonymise**: remove the name and any identifying detail. Check that the combination of details does not identify the client.
- **Index the numbers**: show percent change or indexed values instead of absolute revenue, spend or CPA.
- **Delay**: publish after a period the client agrees to.
- **Switch the source**: use the user's own account, a test account, or public assets (a public ad, a public landing page).
- **Ask for a release**: a short written addendum or email approving the post.

## Atypical results (FTC)

The FTC rule on reviews and testimonials (effective 21 October 2024) requires a clear disclosure of generally expected results when a featured result is atypical. A "results not typical" disclaimer does not satisfy it. If the result is unusually good:

6. Say what a typical result looks like for this kind of account, if the user knows it.
7. Do not imply the result is what a new client should expect.

## Own accounts and public material

8. Confirm the user can show the numbers (own business, own test budget, public data).
9. For teardowns of other companies' public ads or pages, state what is observed and mark inference as inference. Do not state another company's spend or results as fact.

## Output

Record in "Claims to verify": consent status (written, verbal, none, not needed), what was anonymised or indexed, and whether atypical-results framing was added.
