# Writing prompts and interview

Rules to give the user with every set: write it fast, no AI help, no polishing, rough is fine, 80 to 200 words each. Real accounts and real numbers only, and anonymise clients. Dictation is fine; paste the transcript unedited. Typos stay.

Pick 3 to 5 exercises that match the posts the user plans. Run more rounds if the profile is still thin.

## 1. Writing exercises

**Results and judgment (case study, experiment write-up)**
1. Tell a colleague about the last test that surprised you. What you changed, what happened, and what you thought afterwards.
2. A client asks why you paused a campaign that was spending well. Write your answer to them, as you would send it.
3. Describe a result you got wrong at first. What you believed, what the data showed, when you noticed.
4. Explain the one metric you trust least in a Google Ads or Meta Ads account, and why.

**Opinion (contrarian take)**
5. Finish this in your own words: "Most advice about [bidding, creative, tracking, attribution] gets this wrong because..."
6. Name a tool, rule or best practice you ignore. What do you do instead, and what happened when you tried the standard way?
7. What would a peer in your field disagree with you about? Argue your side in a paragraph.

**Teaching (framework, teardown)**
8. A junior asks how you decide where to put the next budget. Write what you tell them.
9. Look at one ad or landing page you saw this week. What did you notice first, and what would you test?
10. Write the three questions you ask before touching a new account.

**Register checks**
11. Write 5 lines as if texting a friend in the industry about a hard week.
12. Write the same result (use any real one) twice: once for a hiring manager, once for a peer.
13. Rewrite your LinkedIn headline or "About" section the way you would say it out loud.

## 2. Interview questions

Ask in batches of 2 or 3. Record answers in the user's words.

1. What do you believe about paid media that most of your peers would dispute?
2. What advice in your field makes you roll your eyes?
3. What was the first account you managed, and what went wrong in it?
4. Which result are you proudest of, and which result do you not talk about?
5. Who do you want to notice your posts: name the kind of person (title, company type), not a person.
6. What do you want that person to think of you after reading three of your posts?
7. Which words or phrases do you never want in a post under your name?
8. Whose writing do you like in this field, and what exactly do you like about it?
