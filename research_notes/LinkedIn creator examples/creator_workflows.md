## 1. How creators collect raw material (swipe files, idea capture, client calls, dashboard screenshots, post-mortems)

### Takeaway
Sources agree on a low-friction capture habit kept apart from drafting, plus a searchable swipe file of structures rather than wording. Client or sales calls are a commonly cited raw-material source. I found no sourced creator workflow for dashboard screenshots or post-mortems on LinkedIn specifically.

### Cited Findings
Workflow (low-risk, easy to try):
- Keep a simple database or spreadsheet (Notion is the example given) of posts that catch your attention, searchable mid-draft. [Search summary] — [3 steps to LinkedIn content](https://the-systematic-sales-leader.beehiiv.com/p/3-steps-to-linkedin-content-no-more-writer-s-block-8e924495eca56d5c); [Taplio on Notion](https://taplio.com/blog/3-ways-using-notion-can-boost-your-linkedin-content-strategy); [Socialkit swipe file](https://socialk.it/en/blog/swipe-file-for-social-media)
- Save the structure or mechanism (promise up front, steps, call to action), not the wording. [Search summary] — [Socialkit swipe file](https://socialk.it/en/blog/swipe-file-for-social-media); [Boutique Growth help article](https://boutique-growth.helpscoutdocs.com/article/77-examples-additional-content-inspiration-swipe-files)
- Separate capture (all day, even ideas that seem weak, in one tool) from creation (a later sitting). [Search summary] — [Tim Keen, content library](https://keendigital.beehiiv.com/p/your-new-content-library); [Wycliffe Opondo newsletter](https://wycliffes-opondo.beehiiv.com/p/never-run-out-of-linkedin-content-ideas-again)
- Call transcripts as raw material: feed only the marked sections of a call to an AI and tell it to keep the client's exact phrasing. [Search summary] — [Lilach Bullock, one sales call into a week of content](https://www.lilachbullock.com/turn-one-sales-call-into-a-week-of-content/)
- Tim Keen describes recording client calls with permission and pulling about five ideas per call. [Search summary, Self-reported single anecdote] — [Tim Keen, "my Airtable writes faster than you"](https://keendigital.beehiiv.com/p/my-airtable-writes-faster-than-you)
- Loop described by one newsletter: pull insights from real sales calls, turn objections and pain points into posts, then use posts that do well to start DMs. [Search summary] — [Content Lab vol 56](https://content-lab-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/the-content-lab-vol-056)
- Vendors sell call-to-post pipelines (Letterdrop with Gong, Taplio with Claude, others). [Search summary, vendor] — [Taplio: calls to LinkedIn posts](https://taplio.com/blog/calls-to-linkedin-posts); [Letterdrop: Gong calls to LinkedIn](https://letterdrop.com/blog/gong-to-linkedin)
- Automation option: an n8n template that pipes admired posts into an Airtable idea vault. [Search summary] — [n8n workflow 3520](https://n8n.io/workflows/3520-turn-your-linkedin-insights-into-content-ideas-with-airtable-and-real-time-linkedin-scraper/)
- Several sources warn that a swipe file alone does not solve shipping; it needs a handoff to drafting and scheduling. [Search summary] — [LinkedInPreview workflow guide, 2026](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-content-workflow-2026)

Ad-account material (adjacent, not creator workflow):
- Agency and vendor teardowns of ads use the free LinkedIn Ad Library; run duration is the only performance proxy, and profitability is unknown. [Search summary] — [Understory Agency](https://www.understoryagency.com/blog/linkedin-ad-library-campaigns-dissected)

## 2. Drafting and editing workflows

### Takeaway
Guides converge on: write or choose the hook, draft fast without editing, then cut filler and swap vague wording for specifics, with formatting polish last.

### Cited Findings
- First 1-3 lines decide whether people expand the post; write 5-10 hook variants and keep the best. Guides give differing preview lengths (about 140 to 210 characters), so check on your own phone. [Search summary] — [LinkedInPreview, how to draft](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/how-to-draft-linkedin-posts); [Supergrow](https://www.supergrow.ai/blog/how-to-write-a-linkedin-post)
- Draft rough, then edit: remove filler, cut weak openings, replace vague phrasing with specifics. [Search summary] — same sources
- Edit trick: find the strongest line in a long draft and move it to the top; delete the first sentence and see if the post improves. [Search summary] — [LinkedInPreview](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/how-to-draft-linkedin-posts)
- Tighten the hook and formatting last, after the substance is settled. [Search summary] — [Letterly](https://letterly.app/blog/how-to-write-linkedin-post/)
- One piece argues AI-written posts need a point, not polish. [Search summary, title only] — [Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/your-ai-written-linkedin-posts-need-a-point-not-polish)
- Voice-first drafting (speak, then clean up) is promoted by a vendor. [Search summary, vendor] — [Ligo Social](https://ligosocial.com/blog/how-to-write-a-linkedin-post)

### Inferences
- A repeatable template for a marketer: claim, the account context, the number or screenshot, what changed, the caveat. This is my suggestion, not a sourced practice.

### Gaps
- No named creator's end-to-end process was found; guidance is mostly vendor blogs.

## 3. Repurposing one insight into a post, carousel and newsletter

### Takeaway
Sources say to start from a strong idea and rebuild it for each format rather than paste it. Framework ideas suit carousels; longer reasoning suits a newsletter.

### Cited Findings
- Rebuild the idea in each channel's native shape instead of copying; start from the best-performing asset, not the newest. [Search summary] — [Koka Sexton, one asset into 20 formats](https://kokasexton.com/?p=4343)
- Extract 3-5 supporting points that become carousel slides or sub-points in a text post; do not cram a whole newsletter into one piece. [Search summary] — [Contentdrips, newsletter to social posts, dated 2026](https://contentdrips.com/blog/2026/09/how-to-turn-a-newsletter-into-social-media-posts/)
- Sample schedule: carousel, newsletter section and short video spread across a week, with a follow-up post in week two. [Search summary, vendor template] — [Averi template](https://resources.averi.ai/templates/content-repurposing-template)
- Works in reverse: a LinkedIn post with unusually strong engagement can be expanded into a newsletter edition. [Search summary] — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/how-to-repurpose-content-for-linkedin)
- Pierre Herubel has a post on 7 repurposing tactics (page not read, content unknown). [Search summary, title only] — [Substack](https://pierreherubel.substack.com/p/my-7-content-repurposing-tactics)

### Inferences
- Suggested sequence to try: text post first (tests the hook), carousel for the framework version, newsletter for the full reasoning with the caveats. [Unverified suggestion]

### Gaps
- Vendor pages promise differing asset counts per idea; none was verified. No named creator's repurposing log was read.

## 4. Commenting and relationship routines

### Takeaway
Most sources recommend a short fixed list of accounts and a daily 10-30 minute routine of substantive comments. All performance numbers come from vendors and are unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Keep a short list, roughly 20-30 accounts, chosen by who your buyers actually read rather than by follower count. [Search summary] — [Koka Sexton, comment engine](https://kokasexton.com/?p=2219); [Salesforge](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/linkedin-commenting-strategies)
- Daily routine: open the list, scan, comment, log; 10-25 minutes; one source suggests 5-10 relevant comments a day. [Search summary] — [Koka Sexton, 15-minute routine](https://kokasexton.com/?p=1980); [Socialkit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/linkedin-comment-strategy)
- Comment early (some say within 30 minutes to 2 hours of the target's post). [Search summary] — [Teract guide, 2026](https://www.teract.ai/resources/linkedin-comment-strategy-2026); [Ciela, dated March 18, 2026](https://ciela.ai/blogs/linkedin-commenting-strategy)
- Setup: saved searches, Sales Navigator alerts and post notifications for key accounts. [Search summary] — [Salesforge](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/linkedin-commenting-strategies)
- Consistency is the recurring theme; inconsistency is the most common reason it fails. [Search summary]
- Automated commenting tools are sold (e.g. PhantomBuster); this conflicts with the "manual, thoughtful" ideal and carries platform-policy risk. [Search summary, vendor] — [PhantomBuster](https://phantombuster.com/blog/ai-automation/ai-linkedin-commenting-tool/)
- Quoted DM-response and profile-visit figures in these pages are vendor marketing; excluded.

### Inferences
- For a performance marketer, the list could mix hiring managers/heads of growth, agency peers and target-client roles, with comments that add a data point or a counter-example. [Unverified suggestion]

### Gaps
- No creator-reported manual-comment log or template was read; guidance is from vendor and agency blogs.

## 5. Keeping voice and specificity when using AI

### Takeaway
Consistent advice: you supply the idea, experience and data; AI helps with structure and wording; use real voice samples; keep human review. Voice tooling details (Projects, Styles, Skills) are vendor tutorials that may have changed.

### Cited Findings
- Reverse the flow: bring the idea; asking AI to write from scratch with no context gives generic output. [Search summary] — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/ai-linkedin-post-generator); [Echai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/r/how-to-write-linkedin-posts-with-ai-that-sound-human--f-r076)
- Write a rough draft yourself and have AI polish, so the thinking stays yours. [Search summary, one creator] — [Social Media Tea](https://socialmediatea.beehiiv.com/p/your-ai-generated-linkedin-posts-sound-like-ai)
- Provide real story, data or opinion; generic openers ("excited to share") and tidy listicles read as nobody's voice. [Search summary] — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-use-ai-on-linkedin-while-sounding-human/); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/chatgpt-prompts-linkedin-posts-dont-sound-like-ai/)
- State explicit bans (e.g. no em-dashes, emojis, specific buzzwords). [Search summary] — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/chatgpt-prompts-linkedin-posts-dont-sound-like-ai/)
- Voice samples: analyse 5-10 of your own best posts, merge into one voice guide, store in a Claude Project's instructions/knowledge, a Style or a Skill; update samples as you evolve. [Search summary, vendor tutorials, verify against Anthropic's current docs] — [DEV: voice-matching with 5 sample posts](https://dev.to/nicolasai/voice-matching-linkedin-posts-with-claude-5-sample-posts-39ma); [Composio](https://composio.dev/content/claude-skills-for-writing-build-a-voice-that-sounds-like-you); [GenAI Unplugged](https://genaiunplugged.substack.com/p/train-claude-brand-voice)
- Keep drafting and proofreading as separate prompts; ask the model to list the top 3 places where tone breaks your guidelines and rewrite only those. [Search summary] — [Composio](https://composio.dev/content/claude-skills-for-writing-build-a-voice-that-sounds-like-you)
- Human review before posting; vendors pitching voice-learning tools have a sales interest. [Search summary]
- Outlines built from your own notes: results did not directly cover this; the closest are the call-transcript workflows in section 1.

### Inferences
- A safe workflow: your raw notes and numbers go in, you ask for an outline only, you write the draft, then use AI for a critique pass against a banned-phrase list. [Unverified, my synthesis]

## 6. Design practices for carousels / document posts

### Takeaway
Document posts are multi-page PDFs; sources stress a specific cover title, early value, consistent design, mobile legibility, and a clear last slide.

### Cited Findings
- Export as PDF; limits disagree across sources (one says under 10 MB, another says 100 MB and 300 pages), so check LinkedIn's current help page. [Search summary, conflicting] — [Oktopost, 2026](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-pdf-best-practices/); [Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-to-post-a-carousel-on-linkedin/)
- Put the strongest material in the first slides; use a specific cover title over a generic one; one guide suggests splitting posts past 30 pages. [Search summary] — [Oktopost](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-pdf-best-practices/); [Yardstick](https://theyardstickagency.co.uk/blog/your-complete-guide-to-creating-and-posting-carousels-for-linkedin-and-why-you-should)
- Consistency over complexity; test on desktop and phone; templates in Canva, PowerPoint or Slides. [Search summary] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/linkedin-carousels/); [TechWyse, 2026](https://www.techwyse.com/blog/infographics/linkedin-carousel-best-practices-2026)
- Links inside the PDF work but the slides themselves are not clickable on LinkedIn; end with a next step such as save or comment. [Search summary] — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-carousel)
- A document/carousel post can be scheduled up to 3 months ahead per one guide. [Search summary, verify] — Oktopost link above
- Screenshot-based creative: one agency argues real product dashboards give clearer evidence than generic visuals. [Search summary] — [Understory Agency](https://www.understoryagency.com/blog/linkedin-ad-examples-b2b-saas-creative-teardowns)

### Inferences
- For teardowns, a repeatable template (cover, context, screenshot with annotation, takeaway, caveat) matches the consistency advice; blur client names and figures. [Unverified suggestion]

### Gaps
- No design guidance specific to marketer teardowns or frameworks from a named marketer was read; sources are generic B2B guides.

### Takeaway
