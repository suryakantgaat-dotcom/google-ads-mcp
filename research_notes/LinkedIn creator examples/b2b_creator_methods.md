# How established B2B LinkedIn creators describe their post formats and writing methods

Scope and honesty note. Only the Buffer blog pages (three creator-authored guides) were fetched and read in full. Almost every other creator host (substack, beehiiv, kit.com, exitfive.com, growthunhinged.com, newsletter.rb2b.com, creatoreconomy.so, buzzsprout, socialmediaexaminer, linkedin.com) was blocked by the session proxy (403 or DNS failure). Those creators are therefore represented only through WebSearch result summaries, marked [Search summary]. Labels: [Read] = primary page read; [Search summary]; [Self-reported] = result the creator claims; [Unverified]. None of this is a claim about the LinkedIn algorithm; where a source talks about the algorithm I record it as that source's opinion only. Dates: 2026 unless stated.

## 1. Which post formats and templates (hook, body, close) do creators describe?

### Takeaway
Creators converge on a story-or-result opening, a body that gives the full value inside the post (no click-away needed), and a light close. Most documented "templates" are really third-party analyses of a creator's posts, not the creator's own template. Only Buffer's team describes formats in first person and with data.

### Cited Findings
- Tamilore Oladipo (Sr. Content Creator, Buffer; says 25k+ followers, posting since 2020) describes hooks as the first line or two before "see more", and cites AuthoredUp's analysis of ~310,000 posts (Dec 2025 to May 2026) ranking opening types: mid-story start, contrarian statement, bold data-backed claim, number or result. Questions ranked last (median engagement 2.16% vs 2.60% for story openers). She adds she would treat any single study cautiously. [Read] [Self-reported / third-party study] — [Buffer, Oct 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-increase-linkedin-followers/)
- Oladipo's own best posts fall into two groups: "something I tried and what happened" and "something I am figuring out in real time" (one became a recurring series). She tells business owners the equivalent: a decision made, a client project that went wrong, a current test. Body voice: write as you would speak to a colleague. [Read] — same page
- Oladipo advises zero-click posts: put the full value in the post, optionally add the link in the first comment. Buffer data she cites: link-preview posts got ~28% fewer impressions and ~44% fewer engagements than the same accounts' non-link posts; URL-in-text about 16% fewer on both; link-in-first-comment ~15% more impressions than no-link posts (flagged by Buffer as only a promising sign). She notes LinkedIn staff said there is no link penalty (Sept 2025). [Read] [vendor data, not independently verified] — same page
- Adam Robinson (RB2B founder). Search summary of his own free course (newsletter.rb2b.com, blocked) says it covers a "3 C's" framework, profile and CTA checklist, sourcing ideas from top creators and Reddit, a connections strategy, and waiting about 60 days before pitching. [Search summary] — [RB2B newsletter](https://newsletter.rb2b.com/p/how-i-hit-140-000-followers-on-linkedin-in-2-years). A third-party newsletter (Catalyst) analysing his hooks reports story-style hooks beat statement hooks, frequent use of numbers, and hooks ending in a colon leading into the body. That is the analyst's reading, not Robinson's words. [Search summary] — [Catalyst](https://gotcatalyst.beehiiv.com/p/adam-robinson-s-22m-linkedin-hook-formula)
- Adam Robinson as described by others: shows messy behind-the-scenes work and candid challenges to humanise the journey. [Search summary, secondary] — [Capitaly](https://capitaly.vc/blog/adam-robinson-retention-com-rb2b-the-linkedin-growth-playbook-for-saas-founders)
- Kyle Poyar (Growth Unhinged; audience is SaaS growth and product leaders), in a Substack note, says his top graphics are original data or research and marked-up screenshots, styled infographic-like so readers draw their own conclusion, and he likes Sankey diagrams; he says zero-click content matters more than links. [Search summary; his claims of 90,000+ followers and 14M impressions in a year are Self-reported and undated in the summary] — [Substack note](https://substack.com/@kylepoyar/note/c-129201712)
- A Saywhat co-founder playbook (name not given in the summary) claims analysis of 100+ top posts and describes a "Behind the Curtain" format showing your real process step by step. [Search summary, vendor] — see search "LinkedIn creator how I write posts framework B2B" results, e.g. [Averi playbook](https://resources.averi.ai/playbooks/linkedin-content-playbook)
- Framework-post template (problem, numbered steps with one sentence each) appears in a B2B posting playbook; useful for saves but it is a vendor template. [Search summary, vendor] — [GTM stack playbook](https://gtmstack-production.pages.dev/playbooks/linkedin-posting-playbook)

### Inferences
- Common skeleton across sources: hook (story, number, or contrarian claim) then a short body (what I tried or did, what happened, the number) then a takeaway or soft invitation, with the link held back. None of the sources I read gave an explicit closing template, so the "close" is thinly evidenced.

### Gaps
- Could not read Robinson's course, Poyar's guide, or Exit Five material (hosts blocked); no first-person hook/body/close templates verified from them.
- No creator-authored close/CTA template found.

## 2. How do creators pick topics?

### Takeaway
Stated methods: choose 3 to 5 topics you can discuss at length, mine your own work and customer conversations, and keep a swipe file of other creators' posts for structure (not copying).

### Cited Findings
- Oladipo: pick 3 to 5 topics; narrow beats broad (her example contrasts "marketing" with "B2B SaaS marketing for early-stage founders"); topics sit where audience pain overlaps what you know. Her colleague Hailley Griffis uses three pillars and says she grew her audience 26% in H1 2025 (self-reported via Buffer). She quotes consultant Melanie Goodman that a focused 8,000 can outperform an unfocused 80,000 (opinion). [Read] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-increase-linkedin-followers/)
- Charu Mitra Dubey (B2B SaaS writer, Buffer guest, published Nov 2023): define an audience persona; use an 80/20 split of niche expertise to broadly relatable posts; keep a swipe file (Notion, Trello or Taplio collections) to study structure, length, headline wording, not to copy. [Read, 2023] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-i-built-my-linkedin-audience/)
- Aarushi Singh (freelance B2B SaaS marketer; Buffer guest, article refers to 2018-2023, exact date not shown): find a point of view from wins, failures, decision-making, a contrarian take, or personal interests as metaphors (she cites a chef-trained marketer using cooking metaphors); expect "first ugly drafts". [Read] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-strategies-grow-to-30k-followers/)
- Founder-focused vendor guides suggest answering the five most common prospect questions from sales calls publicly and turning client calls into posts. [Search summary, vendor] — see [Revboss](https://revboss.com/blog/build-repeatable-linkedin-content-engine-b2b-saas-services-lean-team)
- Robinson's course reportedly sources ideas from top creators and Reddit. [Search summary] — [RB2B newsletter](https://newsletter.rb2b.com/p/how-i-hit-140-000-followers-on-linkedin-in-2-years)

### Inferences
- For a performance marketer: account-level learnings, test results and client questions map directly onto the "something I tried and what happened" pattern.

### Gaps
- No creator described a quantitative topic-selection method (e.g. scoring ideas).

## 3. How do creators write, edit, how long posts take, and how often do they post?

### Takeaway
Creator-stated process detail is sparse. Time-per-post is mostly absent; batching and weekly scheduling appear in vendor content. Cadence advice conflicts.

### Cited Findings
- Oladipo posts about four times a week; Buffer recommends two a week to start. She uses AI for hook variations, tidying and "what to add", but insists the post start from her own experience. She mentions a 2023 experiment of a week of AI-written posts (impressions +11%, engagement +75% vs a typical week; her figures) and says she would not run it the same way now. [Read; Self-reported] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-increase-linkedin-followers/)
- Dubey: schedules posts about a week ahead. Notes LinkedIn had around 3M weekly sharers among 900M+ users in 2023, so a weekly post puts you in the active minority (2023 stat as she cites it). [Read, 2023] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-i-built-my-linkedin-audience/)
- Singh: argues volume-first rules (six posts a week, commenting on 50 posts) are unsustainable; she built a big content machine too early and burned out; advises a sustainable pace; repurposes by (a) different facets of one idea, (b) same content in another format, (c) core message variants, (d) re-sharing older posts; keeps a Canva template for visual consistency. [Read] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-strategies-grow-to-30k-followers/)
- Lilach Bullock (B2B founder audience) says growth came from showing up weekly for about three years rather than going viral or posting daily; says B2B founders want specifics, not inspiration. [Search summary, Self-reported] — [Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-grow-on-linkedin-b2b-founder/)
- Meredith Farley (Substack) data analysis: top follower-gaining posts averaged about 749 characters vs 825 overall; ~11,952 vs ~6,199 average impressions; early engagement correlated with follower gain; video deepened engagement but did little for follower growth. Sample size and method not shown in the summary. [Search summary] — [Farley](https://meredithfarley.substack.com/p/what-actually-drives-linkedin-follower)
- Batching claims (one hour fortnightly to outline posts; Saturday session for a week of posts; 30-40% time savings) come from vendor blogs, not named creators. [Search summary, vendor] — [Contentin](https://contentin.io/blog/how-to-create-7-linkedin-posts-in-under-30-minutes-per-week-real-workflow-revealed/)

### Cadence conflicts
- Buffer data (2M+ posts): more posts per week associated with more impressions per post (11+/week about 17,000 more per post than once weekly) — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-increase-linkedin-followers/) [vendor data, correlational]; contradicted in spirit by Singh and Bullock, who say high frequency is unsustainable or unnecessary — [Singh](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-strategies-grow-to-30k-followers/), [Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-grow-on-linkedin-b2b-founder/).
- Length conflict: Farley top posts ~749 characters vs vendor claims that 1,250-3,000 characters perform best (socialbyidm, a marketing blog) — [socialbyidm](https://socialbyidm.com/linkedin-growth-strategy-from-scratch). Different samples and goals (follower gain vs engagement); unresolved.

### Inferences
- Creators describe "what" far more than "how long": the editing process and minutes-per-post are mostly undocumented in what I could access.

### Gaps
- No creator-stated minutes per post, edit passes, or drafting tools beyond Oladipo's AI use. Robinson, Poyar and Exit Five process pages unreadable.

## 4. Document and carousel design practices

### Takeaway
Advice is mostly from tool and agency pages, not creators; the creator-stated points are: reusable templates, minimal layers, original data graphics.

### Cited Findings
- Oladipo: a carousel is a PDF uploaded as a document post, good for steps, breakdowns, lists; Buffer's 52M-post analysis gave carousels a median engagement rate of 21.77% vs video 7.35%, image 6.52%, link 3.81%, text 3.18%. She admits seven of her eight most-engaged recent posts were video and she posted no carousels. [Read; vendor data] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-increase-linkedin-followers/)
- A CMO of Penfriend.ai (name not in the summary) says he reports 5 million views from carousels, starts from a very simple template and caps layers at two text layers and one image layer. [Search summary; Self-reported] — [Penfriend newsletter](https://penfriend.beehiiv.com/p/my-4-design-rules-for-5-million-views-on-linkedin-2863ffde2d538e0a)
- An unnamed marketer describes going from a research PDF to a finished carousel in under an hour: summarise with ChatGPT, build in Canva, cover images in Ideogram. [Search summary; Unverified identity] — [search listing](https://www.theaireport.ai/partner-columns/how-to-design-viral-linkedin-content)
- Singh: Canva template with a fixed colour palette reused across carousels, banner and video. [Read] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-strategies-grow-to-30k-followers/)
- Poyar: original-data graphics and annotated screenshots (see section 1). [Search summary]
- Format specs conflict: several guides say portrait 4:5 (1080x1350) while others say 1:1; guides vary on words per slide. PDF is the upload format. [Search summary, vendor] — [Oktopost](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-pdf-best-practices/), [Techwyse](https://www.techwyse.com/blog/infographics/linkedin-carousel-best-practices-2026)

### Inferences
- The creator-stated common thread is constraint (template, few elements) plus original data, not elaborate design.

### Gaps
- No creator walkthrough of slide count, cover-slide copy, or design time was readable.

## 5. Stories versus data

### Takeaway
Creators split: personal story and "what I tried" for reach and follows; data and original research graphics for credibility with B2B buyers. The two are rarely presented as exclusive.

### Cited Findings
- Dubey's own comparison (self-reported): a birthday/year-recap post got about 150k impressions, 1.8k+ reactions and hundreds of followers but no leads; an SEO-topic post got five content-writing leads within a week. She uses it to argue for niche posts over generic relatable ones. [Read; Self-reported, 2023] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-i-built-my-linkedin-audience/)
- Oladipo's best posts are experience stories; the hook study she cites favours story openers over questions. [Read] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-increase-linkedin-followers/)
- Poyar leans on original data and charts. [Search summary]
- Farley: milestone posts and concise insights outperformed video for follower gain. [Search summary]

### Conflicts
- Oladipo values relatable personal posts; Dubey cautions they bring reach without leads. Hook advice also conflicts: many guides advise opening with a question; AuthoredUp (as cited) found questions weakest.

### Gaps
- No creator gave a ratio of story to data posts beyond Dubey's 80/20 niche-to-generic split.

## 6. What creators say stops working

### Takeaway
Creator-stated: generic, AI-sounding posts, volume-first tactics, link posts for reach, and relying on the same format. Many "algorithm changed" claims come from agencies and should not be treated as established.

### Cited Findings
- Oladipo: LinkedIn added a "Seems like AI slop" report option in July 2026; she says flagged posts see about 40% fewer views and cites an Originality.AI estimate (~81% of sampled long posts read as likely AI). Treat detector figure cautiously. [Read; third-party figures] — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-increase-linkedin-followers/)
- Singh: quantity-first routines lead to abandonment. [Read]
- Dubey: new-feature advantage fades (her newsletter grew fast when the feature was new; she says fewer people subscribe now). [Read, 2023]
- Agency and vendor claims, not creators: carousels losing edge because overused [Search summary] — [Reeset Substack](https://reeset.substack.com/p/linkedins-2026-algorithm-shift-why); generic AI content stopped working; reach falling to 8-12% of followers (single vendor figure) — see [Informi](https://informi.co.uk/blog/what-do-we-do-now-that-linkedin-engagement-is-dropping). These conflict with Buffer's carousel engagement figure above (different metrics and samples; unresolved).

### Gaps
- No first-person "this format stopped working for me" account from a B2B marketer was found; Exit Five 200k-follower piece could not be read.
