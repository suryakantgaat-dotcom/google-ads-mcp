# LinkedIn post formats and structures for thought-leadership engagement

NOTE ON METHOD: WebFetch was blocked for all hosts tried (authoredup.com, socialinsider.io, buffer.com, theshieldindex.beehiiv.com: DNS not in allowed network domains). All findings below come from WebSearch result summaries, NOT from reading the primary pages. Numbers are therefore second-hand and should be re-verified against the linked primary source before publication. Many sources are vendors with a product to sell.

## Which formats have the best reach/engagement per large datasets?

### Takeaway
Native documents (PDF carousels) and multi-image posts lead on engagement rate in the Socialinsider company-page data (2024 and 2025 data). Personal-profile datasets (AuthoredUp, Metricool, LigoSocial) agree documents are strong but disagree sharply on polls, video and text-only. Treat format rankings as dataset-dependent; no source here is LinkedIn's own data.

### Cited Findings
- Socialinsider 2025 report (2024 data, company pages): multi-image 6.60% engagement rate by impressions; native document 6.10%; video 5.60%. One search summary says the PDF also shows 5.85% for documents, so the document figure is inconsistent within the report. — [Socialinsider 2025 PDF](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/linkedin_benchmarks_2025.pdf)
- Socialinsider 2026 report (2025 data): native document 7.00% (up from 6.10%); multi-image 6.45% (down from 6.60%); multi-image still drives most likes across page sizes. — [Socialinsider 2026 PDF](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/linkedin_benchmarks_2026.pdf); coverage: [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-shows-document-posts-on-linkedin-see-more-engagement/816551/)
- Metricool 2025 LinkedIn study: carousel engagement about 46% above other formats; poll reach up 206%; video described as dominant in the headline. — [Social Media Today press release, Apr 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/press-release/20250402-metricools-2025-linkedin-study-reveals-content-shifts-video-dominates-po)
- AuthoredUp 2025 analysis of 621,833 posts: document/carousel named best content type; polls shown at a 1.64x reach multiplier; video reach multiplier fell from 1.14x to 1.10x. — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin)
- Same search summary reports a later AuthoredUp piece saying poll reach is down 28% (conflicts with 1.64x above; likely different periods, unverified). — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm)
- LigoSocial 2026 study: polls at -50% engagement versus text-only posts. — [LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/research/linkedin-post-formats)
- LinkedIn's own claim (per a search summary, original not located): video generates 1.4x more engagement than other formats. [Unverified: original LinkedIn page not found]
- Document reach claims: dataslayer reports document posts 6.60% engagement vs text posts struggling to break 2%, and "3x higher engagement"; another source says text about 4%; one creator test found text got 5x the carousel's reach (single anecdote). These are vendor/anecdotal. — [Dataslayer](https://dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)
- A "3M-post study" cited in a blog: document/carousel posts 39% more reach and 30% more engagement than average (underlying study not identified). — [Contentin](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/) [Unverified: primary study not located]
- Van der Blom Algorithm InSights 2025 (1.8M posts, 58k profiles, 31k company pages, 12 months to Feb 2025), per secondary recaps: views down 50%, engagement down 25%, follower growth down 59% YoY; saved posts more likely to reach suggested feeds (>60%); fully AI-generated posts about 2.8x less reach and about 5x less engagement. Report is paid and not to be redistributed; I could not access format-level numbers. The Scribd copy appears unauthorized and was not used. — [Richard van der Blom on X](https://x.com/RichardvdBlom/status/1908820478426308656); recap: [Agorapulse](https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/linkedin/linkedin-algorithm-2025/)
- Median reach down about 47% YoY (Dec 2025 analysis, all posts). — [Socialecho](https://www.socialecho.net/en/blog/docs/linkedin-2026-algorithm-report-content-reach-47-percent)
- Newsletters/articles: no official or independent study comparing newsletter reach with posts was found. Vendor claims: 115M+ newsletter subscribers; newsletter engagement up 47% YoY; articles/newsletters are 60% of AI citations from LinkedIn content (attributed to LinkedIn internal data). — [Contentin](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/) [Unverified: claims not traced to LinkedIn]
- Thought-leadership newsletters framed as "depth play, not reach play" in a B2B buyer survey summary (self-reported). — [B2B Lab Report](https://t.me/s/B2BLabReport/20)

### Inferences
- For an authority-focused personal brand, documents/multi-image are the most consistently supported high-engagement formats across 2024-2025 datasets; polls and video are the least consistent and should not be the core strategy.
- Socialinsider data is company pages, so the exact rates may not transfer to a personal profile.
- Saves being a strong signal (secondhand) favors reference-style content (frameworks, checklists, benchmarks).

### Gaps
- Could not read any primary report in full (fetch blocked); format-level van der Blom data, Hootsuite, Sprout Social, Shield Analytics format studies and LinkedIn's own official format guidance were not retrieved.
- No reliable newsletter-vs-post reach data.

## What post length, line-break and formatting patterns are supported by data?

### Takeaway
Longer posts (roughly 1,200-2,500 characters) show higher engagement in several personal-profile datasets, but sources conflict on the exact range. The mobile/desktop "see more" fold (~140/210 characters) is widely cited but is an observed behavior, not a published LinkedIn spec.

### Cited Findings
- AuthoredUp, 372,126 personal-profile posts (Sep 2025 - Feb 2026): posts of 1,301-2,500 characters get 27% higher engagement than posts under 400 characters. — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit)
- Taplio: 2,000+ character posts best performing, peaking at 2.56% (methodology not seen). — [Taplio](https://taplio.com/blog/how-long-should-a-linkedin-post-be)
- Magnettu: best range 800-1,500 characters. — [Magnettu](https://www.magnettu.com/en/blog/what-works-on-linkedin-in-2026)
- LigoSocial cites a LinkedIn analysis of 540,000 posts: 1,200+ characters gets about 3x more engagement (LinkedIn original not located). — [LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/blog/linkedin-post-length) [Unverified: original]
- Shield Analytics newsletter has a post-length analysis; I could not read it. — [Shield Index](https://theshieldindex.beehiiv.com/p/does-linkedin-post-length-actually-matter)
- "See more" fold: about 210 characters desktop, about 140 mobile; line breaks count toward the fold and many short lines can truncate earlier; vendor guides describe it as observed, not official; no hard data on engagement lift. — [BlueCompass](https://www.bluecompass.com/blog/are-your-linkedin-posts-getting-cut-off-find-out-the-best-length); [Contentin glossary](https://contentin.io/glossary/line-break-formatting/)
- Post limit 3,000 characters (includes spaces, emojis, line breaks); comments 1,250; headline 220. — [Eden](https://eden.so/blog/linkedin-character-limits/)
- Buffer: no post-length study found. Buffer published a 2M+ post frequency study (2026) and a 58-post/60-day/568k-impression experiment (content not read). — [Buffer frequency](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/); [Buffer experiment](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-experiment/)
- Consistency: per a recap of van der Blom's data, consistent posters grew followers 61% vs 18% for inconsistent. — [Agorapulse](https://www.agorapulse.com/blog/linkedin/linkedin-algorithm-2025/) (secondhand)

### Inferences
- Target roughly 1,200-2,000 characters for text posts, with the full point of the hook inside the first ~140 characters (inference from converging but vendor-grade sources).
- Short-line formatting helps scanning but can pull the fold earlier; test.

### Gaps
- No controlled data isolating line-break style or emoji use; no primary Buffer/Hootsuite/Sprout length studies obtained.

## Which structures suit B2B/marketing authority content?

### Takeaway
No rigorous head-to-head study of structures (case study, teardown, contrarian, framework, lessons learned) was found. Practitioner sources converge on: specific-number or contrarian hook, evidence-backed body, a reframe, and a discussion-oriented close.

### Cited Findings
- Contrarian takes are recommended as debate-generating but must be backed by real experience or data to avoid reading as clickbait. — [GTM Stack playbook](https://gtmstack-production.pages.dev/playbooks/linkedin-posting-playbook); [99dollarsocial](https://www.99dollarsocial.com/blog/linkedins-latest-trends-for-contrarian-markets)
- Data-led hooks: skeptical B2B audiences respond to concrete numbers; a vendor claims data-point content gets 3.7x more reach with no methodology. — [GTM Stack playbook](https://gtmstack-production.pages.dev/playbooks/linkedin-posting-playbook) [Unverified: 3.7x]
- Teardown pattern: context, then analysis; framework and data templates suit technical audiences better than vague thought leadership. — [GTM Stack playbook](https://gtmstack-production.pages.dev/playbooks/linkedin-posting-playbook)
- Motivational posts are described as declining in 2026 relative to specific/expertise content (opinion). — [Lexiconn](https://www.lexiconn.in/knowledge/blogs/motivational-linkedin-posts-dying-2026.html)
- 360Brew (LinkedIn's LLM-based ranking, per secondary descriptions) reportedly rewards topical consistency/expertise and treats saves as a strong signal; some mechanics (e.g., first-60-token evaluation) come from a single secondary write-up. — [Webtures](https://www.webtures.com/insights/linkedin-algorithm-2026/); [LinkedGrow](https://linkedgrow.ai/blog/linkedin-360brew-algorithm) [Unverified: LinkedIn has not, to my knowledge in these sources, published these details]

### Inferences
- For a performance marketer, account-data case studies and teardowns with real spend/CPA/ROAS figures fit the "specific numbers + evidence" pattern; they also align with reference-worthy (saveable) content. This is inference, not tested.
- Authority audience (hiring managers, clients) favors verifiable specificity over engagement bait.

### Gaps
- No structure-level quantitative comparison found; all structure advice is practitioner opinion.
